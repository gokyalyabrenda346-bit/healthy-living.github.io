Understanding your mind

Your mental wellbeing matters

Mental wellbeing includes how we think, feel, cope with challenges and connect with other people.

Understand your feelings

Give yourself time to identify your emotions without judging yourself. Understanding your feelings can help you decide what support you need.

Create time to recharge

Take breaks, spend time outside and make space for activities that help you feel relaxed and refreshed.

Stay connected

Talking with trusted friends, family members or professionals can help you feel supported during difficult times.

Pause and reset

Take a breathing break

Breathe in gently for four seconds and breathe out slowly for six seconds. Follow the circle at a comfortable pace. Stop if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable.

Everyday wellbeing

Six gentle habits for a healthier mind

Start with one habit that feels manageable. Small actions practised regularly can help support your wellbeing.

1. Check in with yourself

Pause each day and ask yourself how you feel. Naming your emotions can help you understand what you need.

2. Move your body

Walk, stretch, dance or take part in another activity you enjoy. Choose movement that suits your abilities.

3. Stay connected

Make time to talk to a friend or family member. Listening and sharing experiences can provide valuable emotional support.

4. Protect your sleep

Keep a regular bedtime where possible and create a relaxing routine before sleeping. Adequate rest supports overall wellbeing.

5. Take breaks from screens

Step away from your phone or computer when you need a break. Choose activities that help you feel present and relaxed.

6. Ask for support

Speak with someone you trust when life feels difficult. Seeking professional support is a positive step towards caring for yourself.

A gentle reminder
“Take care of your mind the way you take care of your body.”

Your wellbeing deserves time, patience and kindness.

When to seek support

Everyone experiences difficult days. However, if feelings of sadness, anxiety or hopelessness persist, affect daily activities or become difficult to manage, consider speaking with a qualified health professional, counsellor or someone you trust.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, seek urgent help from local emergency services or the nearest health facility.
